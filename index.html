MemoirGrid Privacy Policy

MemoirGrid: Diary & Lock · Developer: Joey El Gemayel
Last updated: 10 October 2026

MemoirGrid is a diary that runs entirely on your phone. This policy is short because there is very little to describe.

  • Your diary — words, photographs, drawings, voice notes, moods, categories — stays on your phone, encrypted. It is never uploaded anywhere by the app.
  • There is no account, no sign-in, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.
  • The only connection the app makes is to Google Play, to sell and check a MemoirGrid Pro subscription if you choose one.
  • The developer receives no data from the app.

What the developer collects

Nothing. MemoirGrid has no account and no server, and the developer never receives your entries, your photographs, usage statistics, crash reports, an advertising identifier, your email address or anything else from the app.

Where your diary lives

Everything you write, draw, photograph or record is stored in the app's private storage on your device, in a database encrypted with SQLCipher. That includes the names of any categories you create: a label can say as much about you as an entry, so they live inside the encrypted diary rather than in the app's settings.

The key to that database is generated on your device and kept in the Android Keystore — in the StrongBox secure element where the hardware provides one. It is protected by your fingerprint or face where your phone supports it, and by the passcode you choose when you first open the app. The key cannot be extracted from the device, by the developer or by anyone else.

The app is excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. Your diary is not copied to Google Drive by the app, and it does not follow you to a new phone unless you back it up yourself.

A few settings — your theme, language, reminder time, whether the home-screen widget is on — are kept outside the encrypted diary so the app can use them before it is unlocked. They contain nothing you wrote.

Backups you make

You can back up your diary to a file, and save that file wherever you choose: your phone's storage, a memory card, or your own Google Drive through Android's file picker. The app never does this on its own.

The backup file is not encrypted. It holds your entries and your photographs and recordings in a form that anyone who has the file can open — that is what lets you restore it on a new phone. Keep it somewhere private. Once you have saved it, the file is covered by the terms of wherever you put it.

The home-screen widget

The widget is off until you add it. If you choose to show a picture or a line of writing on it, the app keeps a copy of that one picture and that line outside the encrypted diary, so the widget can draw it while the diary is locked. You can hide both in Settings, or cover the widget until it is tapped.

Paying for MemoirGrid Pro

MemoirGrid Pro is a monthly or yearly subscription sold through Google Play, with a free trial for new subscribers. The purchase is a transaction between you and Google: Google handles your payment details and account under Google's Privacy Policy, and the developer receives no personal information from it beyond the standard order records Google Play gives every seller. The app learns only whether your subscription is active, and remembers that on your phone for a few days so Pro keeps working without a connection.

You can cancel at any time in Google Play. Writing, reading, searching and backing up your diary stay free whether or not you subscribe.

The app may also show Google Play's own rating card. That card is provided by Google Play and is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

The app asks for no permission to read your photos or files. When you add a photograph or open a backup, Android's own picker hands over only the item you chose.

Deleting your data

Everything the app stores is on your phone. Uninstalling MemoirGrid deletes all of it. There is no account, and nothing held by the developer, to delete. Backup files you saved yourself stay wherever you put them until you delete them.

If you lose access

Because the encryption key is held by your phone's hardware and protected by your biometric and passcode, nobody can recover your diary for you — not the developer, not Google. If you re-enrol your fingerprint the hardware key is invalidated by design, and if you have also forgotten your passcode the diary cannot be opened again. Keep a backup if your entries matter to you; the app reminds you about once a month if you have not made one recently.

Children

MemoirGrid is not directed at children under 13, and it collects no personal data from anyone.
